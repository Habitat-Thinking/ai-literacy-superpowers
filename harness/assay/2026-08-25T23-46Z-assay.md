## Executive summary

The phase was effective and unusually honest about itself. A mechanism that
reported a conclusion it never measured was traced to its evidence, specced
through five revisions under three adversarial passes and one decision-archaeology
pass, implemented with a machine-checked residue assertion, and merged — and both
PR bodies state what the work did *not* do rather than rounding it up. The
repository's own governance corpus was cited as authority inside a design
decision for the first time (`observed`, spec line 131 cites
`HDR-2026-08-25-four-mechanisms-report-the-reassuring-answer-when-they-cannot-determine-the-real-one`).

The single most important opportunity is this: **`HARNESS.md` lost a rule during
this phase, and the governed loop never saw it.** `### Template currency` is the
only heading that left the file (`observed`, heading diff `9a160d6..9995863` —
one deletion, no additions); no decision record accompanied it (`observed`, six
records, none referencing it); and `harness-registrar.py check` reports
`OK (6 decision(s))` afterwards (`observed`). The loop's eight artifacts —
registrar, schema validator, `/harness-propose`, `/harness-review`,
`/harness-accept`, `/harness-check`, `/harness-compile`, `surfaces.yaml` —
contain **zero** occurrences of "garbage collection" or "GC rule" (`observed`,
per-file count). This is exactly the condition assays 2 and 3 each pre-registered
as their corroboration test and each recorded as unmet, so it reaches the loop
layer with two prior observations behind it rather than one incident.

The three open issues do not form one family. **#602 and #606 are the same
shape**: the loop has no door for a legitimate rule change that is not "a
constraint proposed from an assay" — no entry for a mechanically observed drift
finding (#602), no exit for a garbage-collection rule (#606). **#605 is a

## Findings

### finding-1 — A rule left `HARNESS.md` outside the governed loop, and every mechanism reported OK

`### Template currency` was removed from `HARNESS.md` by PR #607, a feature PR.
Diffing the `### ` heading set of `HARNESS.md` between `9a160d6` and `9995863`
with HTML comments stripped returns exactly one line: `< ### Template currency`
— one deletion, no additions, 64 headings before and 63 after (`observed`).
`harness/decisions/` holds six records and none concerns it; the newest record
file predates the phase's first commit by nearly five hours (`observed`, `ls -la`
and `index.md`). Running `python3 harness-registrar.py check` at `9995863`
returns `harness check: OK (6 decision(s))`, exit 0 (`observed`).

The loop cannot express this change. Counting occurrences of "garbage
collection", "garbage-collection" or "GC rule" across the eight artifacts that
constitute the loop — `harness-registrar.py`, `check-harness-decisions.py`,
`commands/harness-propose.md`, `commands/harness-review.md`,
`commands/harness-accept.md`, `commands/harness-check.md`,
`commands/harness-compile.md` and `harness/surfaces.yaml` — returns **0 in every
file** (`observed`). `harness/surfaces.yaml`'s `routes:` block maps
`harness-loop` and `script-validator` to `HARNESS.md` as a whole, with no
section granularity (`observed`). `/harness-review` operates on records that
lapse against an `expires:` field (`observed`, its description and §2), and a GC
rule that predates the loop has no record to lapse.

This is precisely the condition the two prior assays pre-registered. Assay 2's
finding-3 wrote: "If a third assay observes a constraint appearing without a
record, this is its corroborating observation." Assay 3's finding-4 — titled
"No constraint entered or left `HARNESS.md` outside the loop" — diffed both the
`## Constraints` and `## Garbage Collection` sections, found 55 headings
identical, and wrote: "A fourth assay that observes a hand-added constraint has
two prior `no-change` observations to cite" (`observed`, both assays). Both
observations were of the condition *not* occurring. It has now occurred, in the
direction of removal rather than addition.

Two things I am not claiming. First, that the channel was wrong: the spec
records the feature-PR channel as chosen deliberately and story 1's disposition
says so in the human's own words (`observed`). Second, that `/harness-sync`'s
routing defect (#602) is a second instance — I re-observed the two rows at
`commands/harness-sync.md:137-138` and `:205` routing rule-shaped findings to
`/harness-constrain`, but the heading diff shows nothing entered `HARNESS.md`
that way, so nothing was exercised (`observed`). I name it because it is the
same shape from the other direction — no governed entry for a mechanical
observation, no governed exit for a GC rule — and a rule written for only the
exit half will need writing again.

The observable symptom is the one #606 names and I re-confirmed: the decision
index and `/harness-timeline` will show `Garbage collection active:` moving
19 → 18 with no record explaining why, and a reader asking when the project
stopped checking template currency finds the answer only by knowing which spec
to open (`inferred`, from the observed absence of any record and the observed
`OK` from `harness-check`).

enforcement: advisory
surfaces: [claude-code, codex, cursor, copilot, windsurf]

  - HARNESS.md
  - harness/decisions/index.md

#### Proposed rule

````markdown
- **Rule**: A garbage-collection rule may not be added to, retired from, or have
  its **Tool** or **Enforcement** field changed in `HARNESS.md` by a feature PR
  alone. The change carries a Harness Decision Record, and that record's
  `## Rule` block names the GC rule and states which section of `HARNESS.md` the
  change lands in.

  Until a classification routes to `## Garbage Collection`, use `harness-loop`
  with an explicit `target: HARNESS.md` and name the section in the record's
  prose. A GC-rule retirement whose only trace is a row in a deletion table is a
  rule change that `/harness-check` reports as `OK`.
````

#### Cost estimate

One Harness Decision Record per GC-rule change, which means a finding in an
assay first, then `/harness-propose`, then a human writing the cost in their own
words at `/harness-accept` — roughly the 20–40 minutes the existing loop already
costs, added to a PR that would otherwise have deleted eleven lines. It is a
real tax on housekeeping: a GC rule whose script was renamed now needs a record.

Three ways it can be gamed, stated so the approver can price them. (1) Edit the
rule's **What it checks** prose without touching **Tool** or **Enforcement**,
which changes what the rule means while staying outside the rule's own terms.
(2) Retire a GC rule by moving its block inside an HTML comment rather than
deleting it, which leaves the heading count unchanged — this repository already
has one GC rule in that state, *Affordance review staleness*, running in
`gc.yml` while parsing as inactive. (3) Cite an assay finding that says nothing
about the specific rule, since nothing checks the citation's relevance. The rule
is advisory: no validator enforces it today, and a deterministic version — diff
the `### ` heading set under `## Garbage Collection` in a PR and require a new
`harness/decisions/` file in the same PR — would be a separate proposal with its
own build cost. Declaring `validated` here without building it would reproduce
the defect this phase spent five spec revisions removing.

### finding-2 — `/diaboli` sanctions regenerating an objection record instead of disposing it, and the phase paid for it twice

`ai-literacy-superpowers/commands/diaboli.md` step 6 instructs the user: "Fill in
`disposition` and `disposition_rationale` for each objection before proceeding.
The plan-approval gate will not advance while any disposition is `pending`."
Step 7 repeats it: "once all dispositions are filled, present the plan for
approval". Lines 17–18, under *When to use*, name the opposite path as a
supported mode: run `/diaboli` again "when a spec is substantively edited after
an objection record already exists (regenerates the record — old dispositions
are lost; this is intentional)" (`observed`, all four passages).

Nothing in the file distinguishes them. Nothing says that regeneration is for a
spec edited *after* its dispositions were filled rather than *instead of*
filling them.

The phase took the second branch twice. #604's commits are, in order: `8e55729`
objection record (rev 1) → `b17285a` spec rev 2 → `6da8b77` record regenerated
against rev 2 → `fa15047` spec rev 3 → `8d1eae5` record regenerated against rev
3 → `9a9deaf` "Disposition all twelve objections on spec revision 3"
(`observed`). Two full objection records — 24 objections including two criticals
— were generated and discarded with every disposition still `pending`.

The cost is recorded in #604's own review table: revision 1 returned 1 critical
and 5 high; revision 2, which added a script and a `PATH` shim to defend the
notifier, returned 1 critical and 8 high; revision 3 deleted the notifier and
returned 2 critical and 8 high (`reported`, #604 body). The reflection reaches
the same conclusion from the author's side: "Revising against every objection is
what turned a deletion into a script, a `PATH` shim, an HTML-comment grammar in
the governing document, a fourth command disposition and eighteen acceptance
criteria across two revisions" (`observed`, `REFLECTION_LOG.md`).

That the command file's permission *caused* the behaviour is `inferred`, and I
do not lean on it. What is `observed` is that the file offers two incompatible
instructions, gives no rule for choosing, and the more expensive branch was
taken twice in the one phase where we have a cost figure. The reflection
proposes the same remedy as a workflow habit and explicitly declines to propose
a constraint; this finding is the artifact-level half the reflection does not
cover — the text an agent and a human both read before deciding.

I have named `commands/diaboli.md` as target because lines 17–18 are the
specific passage. `skills/advocatus-diaboli/SKILL.md` is the defensible
alternative home, since it is where the disposition vocabulary is defined, and
the approver may move it there.

```yaml
classification: agent-reference
enforcement: advisory
surfaces: [claude-code]
target: ai-literacy-superpowers/commands/diaboli.md
priority: P2
evidence:
  - ai-literacy-superpowers/commands/diaboli.md
  - REFLECTION_LOG.md
  - docs/superpowers/objections/template-currency-measure-content-design.md
overfitting_risk: high
```

#### Proposed rule

````markdown
- **Rule**: An objection record is regenerated only after its dispositions are
  filled. Where a spec is revised in response to objections that were never
  adjudicated, the revision has answered nobody: record the dispositions on the
  record being replaced first, even where the disposition is one word.

  `/diaboli`'s *When to use* must say which of its two instructions applies —
  regeneration follows a spec edit made *after* adjudication, not instead of it.
````

#### Cost estimate

One disposition pass per objection record before any revision — twelve
one-line `disposition` and `disposition_rationale` entries, five to fifteen
minutes, on a record the author has already read. Against the phase's own
figures, that is two such passes bought in exchange for two spec revisions that
were later deleted.

The real burden is not the typing; it is that the author must state *accepted /
deferred / rejected* on an objection before knowing what the revision looks
like, which is genuinely harder than revising first and letting the answer
emerge. Some objections are correct and obvious, and this rule makes the fast
path slower for those.

Gaming is cheap and should be assumed: twelve `accepted` values with a
one-word rationale satisfies the letter of it, and `/harness-board` already
counts pending dispositions but cannot judge rationale quality. Overfitting risk
is **high** — one spec, one author, one phase — and an approver would be within
their rights to decline it on that basis alone and wait for a second instance.
`enforcement: advisory` is stated deliberately: a deterministic version would
have to read git history to know whether a regenerated record's predecessor was
disposed, and this repository has just spent a phase deleting a check that
inferred a conclusion from a proxy.
